  near-zero denominator: `KXFEDDECISION-26SEP-H0` earned `stable` on `rho_full = -0.004471504399603508`

  — or a future run quoting the row — would take for a real surviving lead. This is **L27's lesson applied
  to a correlation instead of a P&L**: a sign/stability check needs a magnitude gate beside it.

  BEFORE sign and retention, because magnitude decides whether those two measure anything), the inflated
  retention still recorded so the artifact is visible, a `magnitude_floor` field on every row, and a printed

  prefix so every existing `.startswith("UNSTABLE")` consumer handles it unchanged.

  lag-ρ *differ* by 0.05, so accepting a smaller *absolute* magnitude as a stable lead is internally
  incoherent (this is the load-bearing reason: the constant is derived from the code, not from the data);
  (ii) |ρ| = 0.05 → R² = 0.0025, a quarter of one percent of next-step variance; (iii) it is deliberately
  LENIENT — a genuine significance bar at this n would be ~1.96/√21 ≈ **0.43** and would kill every ρ this
  window produced, whereas 0.05 kills only the one that is indistinguishable from zero, excluding 0.0045 by
  a factor of ~11 (nowhere near the boundary) while the WC leg's collapsed remnants (0.054/0.053) sit just
  *above* it — i.e. the floor is not placed where it would flatter a past verdict.

### OPEN DEFECT D3 — `signed_leader` maximises SIGNED ρ, so it can name a negative correlation the leader. NAMED, NOT FIXED.

Recorded here as a **named open defect** rather than a fixed one, at the verifier round's direction.

**The defect.** `per_ticker_leadlag`'s leader comparison maximises the **signed** lag-ρ. When both directions'
lag-ρ are negative it therefore names the **less negative** one the leader. That is how
`KXFEDDECISION-26SEP-H0` in the table above got `leader = polymarket` on ρ = **−0.0045**, beating Kalshi's
ρ = **−0.2536**: −0.0045 > −0.2536 as a signed comparison, and "less negative" is not a lead in any economic
reading. A directional-leader test should require the *winning* ρ to be **positive**.

**It is PRE-EXISTING, and it cannot flatter this verdict.** The verifier confirmed via `git show` that this
round's diff only renamed the inline `0.05` to `LEADLAG_SIGNED_LEADER_MARGIN`; the comparison itself is
unchanged context, not introduced here. And its bias runs the wrong way to help: it yields **more**
directional leaders than a sign-gated test would, and the headline is still **0 of 6 survive**. A defect that
inflates the numerator of a claim which came back empty cannot have manufactured the emptiness.

**But the D2 magnitude floor catches it only INCIDENTALLY, and the verifier supplied the concrete
counterexample proving so.** 26SEP-H0 was caught because |−0.0045| happened to fall below the 0.05 floor, not
because its sign was wrong. **`rho_k = −0.30` / `rho_p = −0.20` would clear the magnitude floor comfortably
and could be labelled `stable`** — shipping a "Polymarket leads" claim built entirely on the less-negative of
two negative correlations. The floor and a sign precondition are independent gates; this window happened to
need only one of them.

**The needed fix, and why it is deliberately NOT in this milestone.** A sign precondition on the
leader-declaration itself (refuse to name a leader unless the winning direction's ρ > 0), plus a unit test
pinning the `rho_k = −0.30 / rho_p = −0.20` counterexample to *no leader*. It is not applied here because
changing leader-selection semantics **moves numbers an independent verifier has just confirmed** — the
6-directional-pair structure, the six ρ values and every stability label in §5 — and re-earning that
confirmation is a milestone of its own, not a footnote to this one. `scripts/` and `tests/` were therefore
left untouched in this round. Carried out as lesson candidate #10.
