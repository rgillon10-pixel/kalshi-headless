## 2. Mechanism (unchanged from the registration)

`tape/kalshi_trades/` carries every executed print's aggressor direction in `taker_book_side`.
Signed aggressor flow is the classic short-horizon continuation signal in a limit-order market:
informed takers move price and the move persists. S79 asks whether following that flow pays a
**taker** on Kalshi sports moneylines. Counterparty: slow liquidity providers run over by
informed flow. The registered kill prior leans on the taker/overround wall (a round trip costs
≈2×`TAKER_FEE_RATE` plus the spread) and on S24, which measured these same sports mid-moves
**mean-reverting** (0.454 continuation).

Only the **hold-to-settlement, SINGLE-fee** variant is measurable, and only it was
pre-registered: entry pays one taker fee at the aggressor print price; the position is carried
to the venue's settlement, which costs nothing. Note this makes the test *fairer* than the
prior assumes — it removes one of the two fee legs that prior leans on.

## 7. What this DEAD verdict does — and does not — license

It licenses: **on committed tape, following signed aggressor flow into a hold-to-settlement
taker position on sports moneylines loses money on average and its 95% block-bootstrap CI does
not clear zero.** That is the strategy's registered binding test, run at the spec it was sealed
with, on real executed prices net of the real taker fee. S79's presumptive KILL prior is
confirmed by measurement rather than by assumption.

It does **not** license the following four things, and each is a scope limit, not a hedge. (The
fourth was missing from the first draft and is added as correction C-5.)

**(i) Only the hold-to-settlement SINGLE-fee variant was measurable.** The seconds-to-minutes
round-trip exit that Q54 also names remains **unpriceable**: `tape/orderbook_depth/` supplies
4 captures/day on the trade day (the S9 cadence wall), so there is no fillable exit surface at
that horizon and inventing one would be a synthetic fill price. Note the direction of the
omission: the untested variant carries a **strictly worse** cost structure (two taker fees plus
the crossed spread, versus the one fee charged here). A variant that pays more cost for the same
signal is not a rescue path for a signal that already loses at one fee — but it is formally
unmeasured, and this verdict says nothing about the *short-horizon price path* itself.

**(ii) The minority (NO) arm is n=2 entries in 2 shared units, so the signed-flow conditioning
variable per se is barely identified.** 131 of 133 entries are YES. The headline is
overwhelmingly a statement about the majority (YES) arm — i.e. about "buy YES when 30-minute net
flow clears 10 contracts". This is L312 surviving in weakened form: the sign-variation gate now
technically opens, but at its floor and with both minority units also occupied by majority
entries. The venue's structural 80/20 buy/sell flow asymmetry (L279) means more tape is unlikely
to fix this on its own. Read this verdict as "the flow-following YES taker loses", not as "signed
flow carries no information in either direction".

**(iii) The sample is skewed by the phase-1 backfill selection rule (L314/L315).** The 6 trade
days are not a random or contiguous sample of the venue: phase 1 could afford 17 of 328
identified settled sports games under a declared 40MB cap, ordered round-robin over series and
pulled whole-game-atomic. That cap, ordering and atomicity ARE this CI's sampling frame. The
frame is outcome-blind (selection never read a result), which is what makes the verdict usable at
all — but it skews toward the alphabetically-first game of each league in the earliest days and
toward games heavy enough to survive whole-game atomicity. A DEAD verdict is the direction of
error this frame can least distort (it did not select on returns), yet the CI's *width* is a
property of that frame.

**(iv) The entry is a PRINT-AS-FILL PROXY, and its bias points toward ALIVE.** Every entry assumes
we could have joined an already-executed trade at that trade's own price: there is **no queue
position, no latency, no size cap, and no cost of crossing a wider spread** in this instrument. That
is prime-directive-legal — the price is `broker_truth`, an executed venue print, never a quote or a
midpoint — but it is unambiguously **optimistic for a taker**, who in reality must cross to trade and
may not get the size. Say the direction explicitly: the proxy inflates the measured P&L, i.e. it
biases the result toward ALIVE, so it **strengthens** this kill rather than threatening it. The
corollary is asymmetric and worth stating: a future ALIVE reading built on this same proxy would owe
a fill model before it could be believed; a DEAD one does not.
